# Growth in home prices slowed in September

Home prices increased 1.3% in September compared to the same month a year ago, down from a 1.4% rise the previous month, according to the S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Index. The 10-city composite rose 2% year over year. The 20-city composite increased 1.4% year over year. Chicago reported the highest year-over-year gain among the 20 cities with a 5.5% increase, followed by New York City and Boston, at 5.2% and 4.1% respectively.
Meanwhile, the Federal Housing Finance Agency’s House Price Index showed that home prices were unchanged in September compared to the previous month. The FHFA index is calculated using the prices of houses bought with mortgages backed by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.
